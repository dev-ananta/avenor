# Avenor student survey: findings

**Source:** `SurveyResponses.png` (Google Form export, June 18–25, 2026), transcribed to `survey_transcribed.csv`.

## Method and cleaning
- 34 responses were recorded. One was removed as not genuine (age "926", workload "23:59:59:999:999"). That leaves **33 valid responses**.
- Ages were 14–21 (median 16). 30 of the 33 were 14–18.
- Every respondent who listed a gender put **M**, which matches the paper's focus on young men. The sample is small and self-selected, and most respondents report A grades, so treat these numbers as *our survey*, not national statistics.
- Several column headers are cut off in the export, for example "How often do you f…" and "Which environmen…". Stats from those columns are listed separately below. **Check the full question wording** before you quote them.
- Ranges such as "3–5 hours" were converted to their midpoint. Text answers such as "Couldn't tell ya" were left out of the workload numbers.

## How the findings shaped v03
| Finding | Feature (iOS, Android and web) |
|---|---|
| 39% avoid or distract themselves when stressed | **"Just 5 minutes" starter.** A tiny, low-pressure session lowers the barrier to starting, and Focus Now removes the "where do I begin" decision. |
| 45% game in their free time, and many want help focusing | Full-screen Study Mode, hold-to-quit, a "come back" nudge, and break tips that steer away from short videos |
| ~1 in 4 want something calming | A **60-second breathing reset** on every break |
| Wanting help with planning and procrastination | Quick add, auto-prioritizing, daily ring, kind streaks |
| The paper finds AI chatbots can miss signs of crisis (Wells, Stanford HAI, 2025) | Avenor has **no AI counselor**. It points students to people instead: the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline and trusted adults. |

## Supporting statistics from the research paper
These come from Aoun Jawa, *AI as a Supplemental Tool to Mitigate Mental Health Effects in Men* (in this folder). Cite the original study, as listed in the paper.
- Graduate students experience anxiety and depression at **two to six times** the rate of the general population (Wildey et al., 2022, *Journal of American College Health*).
- During COVID-19, **nearly half** of people aged 19–29 reported symptoms of anxiety or depression, a higher rate than other age groups (Mojsa-Kaja et al., 2023, *PLOS ONE*).
- "Social connection is a significant predictor of longevity and better physical, cognitive, and mental health" (U.S. Surgeon General, 2023).
- AI therapy chatbots failed to recognize suicidal intent in testing (Wells, Stanford HAI, 2025).

> **Wording guardrail:** Avenor is a study and time-management tool, not a mental-health treatment. Say "helps students manage schoolwork stress," never "treats anxiety" or "improves mental health."
